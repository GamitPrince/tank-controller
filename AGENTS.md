## 0. How you work here

1. You only work from a **feature spec** (`docs/stories/S-0xx-name.md`) that belongs to a GitHub story issue `S-0xx`. No spec, no story issue: no work. Do not "set up the whole project" in one go; scaffold only what the current spec asks for.
2. Before editing: `git status`, `git config user.email` (must be `princ3gamit20561@gmail.com`, user.name `GamitPrince`), and `git pull` on `develop`.
3. Branch from `develop`: `feature|bugfix|hotfix/S-0xx-short-description`. Never push to `main` or `develop`; the rulesets reject it.
4. Small commits `type(scope): description`. PRs under about 300 changed lines; split bigger work.
5. Open the PR with the template, `Closes #N`, all 7 checklist items ticked honestly. Do not tick an item you did not do.
6. If anything is unclear, unsafe, or outside the spec, ask. Never guess on safety, phase, or hardware.
7. Never disable, skip, or weaken a CI check, ruleset, CODEOWNERS entry, or test to get green.
8. At the end of a task, report: what changed, what was tested and how, what was NOT done, and any assumption made (mark assumptions `[Confirm]` in code comments or the PR).

## 1. Hard rules (safety and phase)

- **Phase 1 = read-only monitoring of level and temperature only.** No control, no actuation, no AI/analytics, no other sensors (vibration, noise, exhaust, fuel, power are Phase 2), nothing wired into the hardwired safety chain.
- The hardwired safety chain (high-high level trip, low-low heater cut-off, over-temperature trip, emergency stop, pump dry-run, fire/gas) is only ever **read** as status-only digital inputs. Never write code or design a path that outputs to it. All interface designs are input-only.
- If a request touches trips, interlocks, safe states, watchdog, or would blur a phase boundary: stop and ask before building.
- Changes in `firmware/src/safety/` or `docs/safety/` need the EE's approval (CODEOWNERS) and the `safety` label. Any defect in a safety function is Critical.
- The dashboard is read-only for plant data. It never sends commands to devices. The controller is send-only (devices publish; the cloud never commands them in Phase 1).
- Phases: 1 sense/transmit/see; 2 control + new sensors + diagnostics + carbon; 3 predictive heating/energy optimisation; 4 fleet intelligence. (These are not the playbook's 12 SDLC phases.)

## 5. Plant facts (confirmed)

- Heat source: diesel-fired burner heating the mixture in the baghouse; exhaust goes through an existing filtered chimney (exhaust sensors are Phase 2, above the filter). No burner return line (single fuel flow meter, Phase 2). No hazardous zones. Carbon emission is for client monitoring only. No automatic stop on severe vibration.
- Client may or may not provide Ethernet; Wi-Fi may be available. Hot, dusty, vapour-laden outdoor site, ambient up to about 50 C, enclosure IP65.
- **Phase 1 signals (confirmed by the EE 20 Nov 2026; full detail in `docs/stories/P1-03-signal-list-v1.md`).** Analogue: 4-20 mA on the AD4111, two of four channels used — **channel 1 radar level, channel 2 pressure level** (the secondary level measurement, read as a raw value only; comparing it against radar is a Phase 2 mismatch check). Each level source also has its own high and low alarm contacts on the digital inputs. Temperature: **six** 3-wire PT100s on one MAX31865 — two upper, two middle, two bottom, each pair mounted **180 degrees apart** across the tank; separate chip select per channel with common SCK, MOSI and MISO. The paired sensors are read and reported independently; comparing them is Phase 2. Digital: **15 of 16** optocoupler channels used, channel 16 spare — 1 high-high level switch, 2 low-low level switch, 3 inlet valve status, 4 inlet pump status, 5 outlet pump status, 6 outlet valve status, 7 mixture motor on/off, 8 base heater on/off, 9 main heater on/off, 10 low pressure level alarm, 11 high pressure level alarm, 12 low radar level alarm, 13 high radar level alarm, 14 low mixture temperature alarm, 15 dry run alarm. Channels 1-2 and 10-15 are **normally closed** so a de-energised device or broken wire reads as alarm, never as healthy, with 200 ms debounce; channels 3-9 are **normally open** with 5 s debounce (decided 20 Nov 2026). Channels 1-2 and 10-15 are safety-chain or alarm reads and belong in `firmware/src/safety/`; channels 3-9 are equipment status and do **not**. Channels 1-2 are read by story P1-05, 10-15 by P1-09, 3-9 by P1-27. Do not harmonise the two contact senses: normally closed on safety and alarm channels makes a broken wire read as alarm, normally open on equipment status makes open mean "not running".
- **NOT present in Phase 1, do not expect or invent these:** over-temperature trip and emergency stop (they exist at the site but are **not routed to this board**), fire/gas, ambient temperature, wind, thermal oil, heater sheath and heater pocket sensors. A screen or record must not imply a signal we do not monitor. **Channel 16 stays spare** (decided 21 Nov 2026): wiring over-temperature or emergency stop into it was considered and deliberately not done in Phase 1. **PT100 class: take Class B** (decided 21 Nov 2026, provisional), so the accuracy target stays **±2.0 °C** end-to-end; if the delivered sensors turn out to be Class A it can be tightened to ±1.0 °C. The product temperature range is **0 to 200 °C** on all six channels (decided 26 Nov 2026); readings outside it are flagged `out-of-range`. Sensor models and counts for Phase 2 sensors are undecided.
- **Hardware status (reported by the tech lead, 26 Nov 2026):** the MEAN WELL DDR-15G-5 24 V to 5 V converter, the two 4-20 mA inputs (still read by the AD4111), the six PT100s with six MAX31865 modules (shared SPI, six chip selects) and the 16 optocoupler inputs at 24 V DC were tested and EE-approved, and are connected to the FRDM. FRDM-to-ESP32 end-to-end testing remains. The test records and EE approvals are private and never go in this repo.
- **Acquisition (decided 26 Nov 2026):** one consolidated flash record per second; PT100 sampled every 1 s, analog every 100 ms, digital inputs asynchronous with a 10 ms response target (debounce unchanged). Planning figure 86,400 records a day, to be checked against real record size and usable flash. The FRDM acquires and logs from boot whether or not a network is up; the ESP32 connects on its own and the backlog goes out when it can.
- **Density-based mass is removed from Phase 1** (26 Nov 2026): no density table, S-037 closed. Level % and volume stay in the cloud.
- Intended customers: Siemens or Astec (large industrial): fleet scale and enterprise security matter.

## 7. Coding standards

Follow the language style guide; no hardcoded values (constants or config), no commented-out code, no debug output left in, functions about 40 lines or fewer, single responsibility, self-documenting names, comments only for non-obvious logic, `TODO(S-0xx): ...` with a tracked story only. Failed sensors are flagged and excluded, never silently replaced.

## 8. Definition of Done

All acceptance criteria in the spec met; tested on simulator or bench; unit tests passing at or above 80%; no open Critical/High bugs; docs and CHANGELOG updated; safety story: EE approval and test result recorded. AI-written code must still be readable and reviewed by a human; write for the reviewer.
